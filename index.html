Phishing Red Flags to Remember
🔍
Always verify the sender domain
Attackers use look-alike domains. intglobal-security.com is NOT the same as intglobal.com. Always check carefully.
⚡
Urgency is a classic red flag
"Act within 24 hours or lose access" is a pressure tactic. Slow down — legitimate IT never rushes you like this.
🔗
Hover before you click any link
Always hover over links to preview the real destination URL. If anything looks off — don't click it.
🔐
Never enter credentials via email
Legitimate IT systems never ask for your password through an email link. Go directly to the official portal instead.
📎
Treat all attachments with suspicion
Unexpected attachments — even from known senders — can carry malware. Always verify before opening anything.
📣
Report — don't just delete
If you suspect phishing, report it to the security team immediately. Deleting protects only you. Reporting protects everyone.
Could You Have Spotted It?
🔎 Hidden Clues in the Phishing Email Click each to reveal
The email came from it-support@intglobal.com — not from google.com or workspace.google.com. Google never sends password expiry emails from third-party domains. Always check the full sender address, not just the display name.
The email used phrases like "expires in 24:00:00" and "account will be suspended". Google's real password policy emails are calm and give 7+ days notice — they never show a ticking countdown or threaten immediate suspension.
Hovering over the "Reset My Password" button would have revealed the link pointed to http://10.0.12.99 — a raw IP address, not a Google domain. Legitimate Google links always go to accounts.google.com.
The fake login page used http:// not https://. Your browser would show "Not Secure" in the address bar. Google never asks for credentials on an unencrypted page. Always look for the padlock before entering any password.
The fake reset page asked for your current password before setting a new one. Google's real password reset flow uses identity verification (2FA, recovery email, or phone) — it never asks you to type your old password on an external page.
